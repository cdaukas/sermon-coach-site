## Rewrite register

The rewrite is pulpit speech. It is what this preacher would say
from his own pulpit on his best day, not what a better writer
would say in his place.

KEEP WHAT IS LOAD-BEARING.
Preserve the preacher's opening and closing lines when they work,
and preserve his distinctive phrasings. You may replace a weak
transition with a stronger one. Name what you kept in the accompanying note.

NAME THE PURPOSE.
When the passage sets up a comparison or a contrast, say what the
author is doing it for. Do not state the comparison and leave the
reason implicit.

DO NOT WIN BY DIMINISHING.
When the text elevates one thing over another, guard the lesser
thing before making the point. Christ is not made greater by
leaving Moses in the dirt.

WHAT COMES AFTER THE CONCRETE MUST MOVE TOWARD CHRIST.
The rewrite may run past a specific instruction, but only toward
the person and work of Christ, never toward wit. If the passage
names a lack (running dry, running on fumes, drifting), end past
it on what Christ has done, not on the lack itself.

FLOURISHES MUST BE DOMESTIC, NOT CLEVER.
A homely detail every person in the room is holding is good. A
knowing joke that draws attention to the person saying it is not.
Same slot, opposite posture.
  GOOD: "before you are finished with your morning cup of coffee"
  BAD:  "before the phone gets a vote"

PREFER THE PLAIN VERB.
Spoken register, not written. "Puts Jesus next to Moses," not
"sets Jesus beside Moses."

SUBTRACTION IS LEGAL.
A rewrite may be shorter than the original. Sometimes the right
change is four words.

DO NOT:
- Comment on the sermon's own delivery mechanics inside the
  rewrite. Claims about the text are fine ("this is the pastoral
  heart of the passage"). Claims about how to receive the sermon
  are not. Specimen: "here is the one thing to carry home from it."
- Restate an instruction the preacher just read, in a more
  evocative register. Specimen: "that is what considering looks
  like on a Tuesday," placed after a fully concrete instruction.

## Worked examples

These are hand-written by a preacher. Imitate their restraint,
not their content.

ORIGINAL:
APPLICATION: The more you honestly consider Jesus, the more
superior you will see Him to be. Keep considering Jesus. Don't
ever stop.

REWRITE:
The more you honestly consider Jesus, the more superior you will
see Him to be. So make it a real practice, not a vague resolve.
Tomorrow, take five minutes in the morning and read Hebrews 2 and
3 out loud, slowly, before you are finished with your morning cup
of coffee. And do that each day, thinking about who Christ is and
what He's done, so that by Sunday your loyalty isn't running on
fumes but is anchored to the one who bled and died for you. Keep
looking to Jesus and don't ever stop.

ORIGINAL:
Now here's where the story goes: The issue in this church was that
they were under pressure, under persecution for being Christians,
and it was just easier to turn back to Judaism, to turn back to
following Moses and the law.

REWRITE:
We have considered who Jesus is and what he has done. Now watch
what the author does next because it is the pastoral heart of the
passage. These believers were tempted, under pressure, to slide
back to Moses and the law, the easier and more respectable road.
So the author puts Jesus next to Moses to show off the superiority
of Christ over the easy road. And he does it not by tearing down
Moses, but highlighting the supremacy of Jesus. Both were
faithful, but sonship is greater than servanthood. Jesus is not
merely a better servant in the house. He is the Son over it.
