Why I built this

Concerned about AI and preaching? Good. So am I.

I have preached for twenty five years, and I am not looking for something to do the work for me. Neither should you be. I believe the hard work of prayer and study and wrestling with the text is what the Spirit uses to form the preacher, not just the sermon.

So I built The Sermon Coach around a narrower question. Could technology help preachers see what they actually wrote, without touching any of the work that formed them?

What it will not do

It will not write your sermon, and it will not study the passage for you.

It will not tell you what God is saying. It does not know, and neither does anything else you can buy.

It will not replace your judgment about the people who will be sitting in front of you on Sunday. It has never met them.

And it will not tell you whether you were faithful. That is not something software can measure, and anything that claims otherwise is selling you something.

What it does

It reads the sermon you already wrote.

It evaluates that sermon against eleven criteria I built out of Bryan Chapell, Tim Keller, John Piper, Haddon Robinson, the Simeon Trust, and 9Marks. The criteria are mine. The convictions underneath them are not, and I have named where they came from.

It shows you the sentences in your own manuscript that led to each observation, so you can check the work rather than take its word for it.

It tells you what is working, which is the part most preachers never hear specifically.

It puts the growth areas in order, so you know which change is worth making first.

And it keeps your evaluations together, so you can see what your preaching is doing over months instead of on one Sunday.

What I believe

Preaching can improve and is worth developing.

The preacher is responsible for the work.

Scripture is the authority and technology is not.

Feedback makes preachers better, and most of us are not getting any.

One sermon can teach you something, and a body of sermons can show you a pattern.

Technology can serve the preacher without becoming the preacher.

Never let a tool become a replacement for the Holy Spirit.

You do not have to agree with it

The evaluation is an assessment, not a verdict. Read it, take what is useful, and throw out the rest. You know the text, the room, and what that Sunday was for. It does not.

I ran my own sermons through it before I showed it to anyone. It found things I had missed, and I knew it the second I saw them. That is roughly what a good editor does.

Dr. Christopher M. Daukas
