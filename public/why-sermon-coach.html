AI and preaching

AI should help you see your preaching. Not replace it.

I have preached for twenty five years, and I am not looking for something to do the work for me. Neither should you be. The hard work of prayer, study, and wrestling with the text is what the Spirit uses to form the preacher—not just the sermon.

So I built The Sermon Coach around a narrower question:

Could technology help preachers see what they actually wrote, without touching the work that formed them?

What it won’t do

It won’t write your sermon.

You do the study, the wrestling, the writing, and the preaching.

It won’t tell you what God is saying.

Scripture is the authority. Technology isn’t.

It won’t know your people.

You know the people sitting in front of you on Sunday. It doesn’t.

It won’t judge your faithfulness.

Faithfulness isn’t something software can measure.

What it does

It reads the sermon you already wrote.

  1. 01
    It evaluates your sermon against the standard.

    Eleven criteria built from established convictions about faithful, clear, Christ-centered preaching.

  2. 02
    It shows its work.

    See the sentences in your own manuscript that led to each observation, so you can examine the feedback rather than simply take its word for it.

  3. 03
    It tells you what is working.

    Most preachers hear what needs fixing. Sermon Coach also shows you what you’re already doing well.

  4. 04
    It prioritizes your growth.

    Your growth areas are put in order, so you know which change is worth making first.

  5. 05
    It tracks your development.

    Keep your evaluations together and see what your preaching is doing over months—not just on one Sunday.

What I believe

  • Preaching can improve and is worth developing.
  • The preacher is responsible for the work.
  • Scripture is the authority and technology is not.
  • Feedback makes preachers better, and most of us are not getting any.
  • One sermon can teach you something. A body of sermons can show you a pattern.
  • Technology can serve the preacher without becoming the preacher.

Never let a tool become a replacement for the Holy Spirit.

You don’t have to agree with it.

The evaluation is an assessment, not a verdict. Read it, take what is useful, and throw out the rest. You know the text, the room, and what that Sunday was for. It doesn’t.

That’s roughly what a good editor does.
